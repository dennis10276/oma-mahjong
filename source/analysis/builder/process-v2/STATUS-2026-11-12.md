# Level making: where it stands (Claude, 2026-11-12)

Nothing here changes the game, main, pins, bots or gate thresholds. Proposals only.

## What was wrong with every builder so far
Each builder (G5 visible choice, G7-G8 recipes, builder-next G9-G13) built one board for one difficulty request
and failed if the measured difficulty missed by more than 1 try. Measured on 5,366 generated readable boards:

- **The family bot's ratings split in two.** Of boards that pass the static checks, about 92% rate under 7 tries
  (most under 3), and most of the rest rate 20+. Roughly 1% land on the curve (9-20). The target range sits on a
  cliff, which is why small changes swing a level from easy to near-impossible.
- **Many "hard" boards are hard on the clock's edge only.** On 20 hard boards, adding clock moves one at a time:
  about 8 drop from the cap to 3-5 in a single step, about 5 come down gradually, about 7 stay flat (their
  difficulty is in the board). Data: `source/build/bank/ladder.jsonl`.
- **Above about 13 tries the bot is mostly repeating one blunder.** Of 115 boards that reached the play-trace
  checks, 97 have a repeat-blunder share (B7) of 0.3 or more; their median rating is 27.8. The 18 with a low
  share rate 6.4-18.7 (one at 30.9). The skilled bot wins most of the high ones first try.
- **The skilled band blocks most of what is left.** B3 wins 92% of readable boards on the first try. Of 132
  boards with stable family difficulty of 7+, 15 are in the 2.5-6 band and 17 are above 6.

## The new process: bank and place
Tools in `source/tools/builder/bank/`.

1. `generate.js` makes puzzles with **no difficulty target** and filters cheapest first: construction proof,
   static checks, family difficulty (drop under 7), **stability** (drop a board whose difficulty collapses with
   1-2 more clock moves), full measurement on fresh seeds (skilled 80 campaigns, family 160, cap 40, raw tries
   kept), skilled above 6 rejected, play-trace checks. Survivors go to `source/analysis/builder/bank/puzzles/`
   with a card. `par.js` runs it on up to 8 cores inside one heavy job.
2. `place.js` fills levels from the bank on Dennis's curve: within 1 try per level, five-level windows within
   0.5, 96 tiles only on tens, one use per puzzle, no repeated matching body, skilled 2.5-6 unless a level is
   explicitly excepted. Unfilled levels stay holes, and it prints what the bank lacks.
3. `summary.js` reports the funnel, cost and which settings produce which difficulty.

Steering is statistical. From 1,956 measured boards: tray room 0 gives 20% at 7+ against 2% at tray room 2; the
ice-lock-clock story 18%; the two room-in-tray stories under 1%.

## Result of the first run (8 cores, 90 minutes, 3,739 candidates)
12 puzzles banked, 2.3 per CPU-hour.

| Family difficulty (cap 40) | Banked | Skilled band 2.5-6 | Low repeat-blunder share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6-12 | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| 13-21 | 3 | 0 | 0 |
| 27-40 | 5 | 0 (one at 2.35) | 0 |

Placed on 250-279: 1 of 30 levels under Dennis's rules, 3 of 30 with the skilled band dropped.
So the tooling works end to end, but with today's rules and rulers the bank fills slowly at 9-12 and has nothing
honest above 13.

## Decisions for Dennis
1. **The curve's scale.** "Family about 10 at 250, 19 at 325" on the bot scale is twice what is live at 230-239
   (about 5), and above 13 the bot's number is mostly its own repeated blunder. Options: (a) keep the curve and
   accept that 13+ has no supply yet; (b) start 250 near 7-8 and rise to about 12 by 300, where honest puzzles
   exist. Recommended: (b).
2. **The skilled band 2.5-6.** B3 and Dennis's real results agree that readable boards are a first-try win for
   him. Keeping the band leaves about 0.6 puzzles per CPU-hour. Options: keep; lower the minimum to 1.5; drop
   the minimum and keep only the maximum of 6. Recommended: keep the maximum, drop the minimum, and judge
   "hard for Dennis" from his real tries.
3. **Make the repeat-blunder check (B7) a requirement** (it is report-only now). Recommended: yes, maximum 0.3.
4. **Whose tries the curve means.** The yardstick weights Dennis x2, so it sits near his 1-2 tries while Gerda
   needs 7. Should the curve follow the normal players?
