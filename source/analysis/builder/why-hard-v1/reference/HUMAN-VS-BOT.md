A uniquely safe state exists in every sampled easy and brutal board with both keys visible. Its presence alone does not explain the difficulty gap. The16-board Task10 opening refinement finds at least two proved winning openings on every board; the sampled brutal boards instead have3–9 proved uniquely safe states along the stored way. These are path-specific lower counts when other states remain unresolved.

The family errors usually have a known-picture winning alternative (easy123/129, middle225/226, brutal928/928). This shows that lack of picture information is not the whole explanation, but it does not prove the safe action was obvious. On brutal boards,400/928 first errors start a free face-up pair too early, and387 leave the stored clock route. A different winning route may exist, so leaving the stored route is descriptive, not proof the tile is always unnecessary.

The honest conclusion is mixed: engine-proven difficult choices and delayed consequences, plus measured memory/policy limitations. There are no new human play results on these research boards. A capped20 is a bot measurement, not a human forecast.
