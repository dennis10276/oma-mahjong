## Checks (2026-11-15, Claude)
- Each narrowed board, re-checked by `narrow_final.js` without the tool that made it: same pictures, specials, face-down tiles and clock
  number; stored route wins by strict replay; the engine's shortest clock route equals the released one; no special face-down; at least
  58 px and at most eight rows; at most six wide (Dennis's own layouts 252, 254, 257, 272 excepted: 254 is 6.5 wide).
- Each also carries the full certificate of Codex's checker (`narrow_proof.certify`: trap cover/thaw relations, special prerequisites and
  lock/ice effects, authored bait prices, readable badges, no new visible twins, own trap proof where the board has one).
- All 15 played to a win in the real game on the 392 x 730 reference phone (`browser.json`), with the held tray-margin UI of
  `oma-mahjong-main` branch `wip/60px-board-review`.
- Layers are the released heights: the narrowing keeps the upper trap where it was and puts moved tiles no higher than layer 3.

## Why the other 25 stay as released
The base layer of these piles is 7 wide and the layers above sit half a tile inward. Pulling the base in by half a tile (the way that
worked for 255, 256, 259, 263, 265, 287, 293) changes which trap tiles lie on which whenever a trap tile sits in the base under two
trap tiles; peeling an outer side fails because the trap or the route to the clock reaches the edge. Where a narrowed shape was found,
it failed a check (clock route, bait price, own trap proof, twins). Not proven impossible; these searches were bounded.
253 had a certified version 6.5 wide; it is left out because of the six-wide rule.
