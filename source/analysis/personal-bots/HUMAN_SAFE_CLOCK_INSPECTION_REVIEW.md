## Optional implementation and production proof

The public_safe_closed_inspection helper uses only current public state. An
unknown closed tile's first tap cannot increase tray occupancy. It removes at
most two tiles if an open twin could match, at most one if only the tray could
match, otherwise zero. With an active clock on another tile, remaining clock
moves must strictly exceed that worst-case cost. Tapping the clock itself is
allowed when its remaining moves are positive. Current takeability is required;
no hidden picture, deck, seed or future observation enters the test.

safeClockInspection:true extends the existing discovery stage to clock positions
that satisfy that bound. The candidate will use adopted19's tray limit2. In
recovery mode, native known-win, safe-clock-exit and constructive tray-recovery
protections remain in their original position and retain priority. The two
public eligibility features are tray occupancy and worst-case inspection clock
margin; this is a safety condition, not a fitted claim of campaign benefit.

Production controls cover1480 hidden-picture cases in40 publicly identical
groups, including ordinary pictures and Joker, tray0-3, open twins and urgent
clocks. All1073 certified cases survive, and each of11 rejected public contexts
has an actual clock-loss counterexample. There are4442 independently replayed
taps. Another74 real-controller controls verify enabled/disabled behavior,
urgent-clock rejection, known-win/clock-exit protection and flag validation;
the selected safe taps independently replay111 additional taps. The full
125-test suite passes16s(native43564 exit0).

This proves the stated one-tap bound and controller protections in those
controls. It does not prove that inspecting is strategically wise on a clock,
that it leaves a route to the clock, or that it improves a real board.
